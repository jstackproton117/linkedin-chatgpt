# Brand Voice Guide — Joe Stackpole

## Positioning
AI-forward Head of Engineering who designs systems, teams, and workflows that reduce friction and increase delivery velocity.

## Tone
- Clear, direct, practical
- Confident but not arrogant
- Builder mindset: "here’s what I tried / learned / changed"
- Avoid buzzword soup
- No emojis (optional rule; enable if you want)

## Content pillars (pick 1 per post)
1) AI-augmented engineering execution (multi-agent workflows, QA loops, velocity with governance)
2) Engineering leadership (team design, accountability, decision-making, hiring/expectations)
3) Architecture & systems thinking (tradeoffs, reliability, security, scaling)
4) Operational transformation (process fixes that compound)
5) Career lessons (leadership growth, role transitions, clarity)

## Red lines (do not post)
- Anything confidential at GetCHKD or prior employers
- Internal incidents or sensitive security details
- Private financials, customer data, legal details
- Naming vendors/partners unless public and positive/neutral

## Sound like a person, not an AI
- Ground claims in specifics from the source: a number, a name, a date, a direct quote. Never vague
  uplift ("this could improve efficiency", "this matters for the future of AI") -- and never invent a
  specific that isn't actually in the source article.
- Banned pattern: "Engineering leaders should/can [verb] to [benefit]." If a sentence fits that
  mad-lib, rewrite it. This is the single most common AI tell in this pipeline's drafts.
- Banned phrases: it's worth noting / in today's landscape / as we navigate / importantly /
  furthermore / it is important to / it's no secret / in conclusion / unlock / game-changer /
  double-edged sword / let's dive in / at the end of the day.
- Short, declarative sentences by default. Fragments are fine for emphasis. Vary rhythm -- not every
  post needs to be hook + 3 bullets + close; let some breathe as a single unbroken argument.
- Minimal hedging. Say the thing. If genuinely uncertain, say so plainly instead of softening with
  "could" / "might" / "arguably".
- Close with an actual opinion, not generic engagement bait ("What are your thoughts?", "Let's
  discuss below").
- Tone reference (borrow the rhythm, not the subject or persona): inthenicoftime.us -- short punchy
  lines, declarative confidence, specific verifiable claims, paired contrasts ("No X -- just Y").
