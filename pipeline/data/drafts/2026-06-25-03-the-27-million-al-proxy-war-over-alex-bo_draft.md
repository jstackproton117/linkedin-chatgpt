Foster inclusivity for a more innovative and successful team. It sounds soft until an AI product reaches users, regulators, customers, or the public.

The mistake is treating AI development as just a technical exercise: pick a model, wire the workflow, test the happy path, then ship the feature.

But the impact shows up later.

Teams miss edge cases. Product assumptions go unchallenged, and automation replaces judgment where it still matters. The people closest to the work get involved after architecture is already set.

The June 2026 Verge coverage of NY-12 showed how quickly AI can become a public trust issue.

To fix this, build diverse review into the execution system from day one.

What I do now:

Ask who is affected before asking what can be automated.
Include non-engineering perspectives to prevent workflows from hardening too early.
Treat objections as design input rather than delivery friction.

Inclusive AI development isn't about slowing engineering down; it's about reducing rework, avoiding blind spots, and building systems that can withstand real users, incentives, and scrutiny.
