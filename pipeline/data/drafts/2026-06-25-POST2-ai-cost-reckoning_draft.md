# POST 2 — The AI cost reckoning
# Sources: The Register (database vendors / AI costs) + The Register / Gartner (coding agents)
# Template: A — Lesson → Principle → Action
# Pillar: AI-Augmented Engineering Execution

---

Two pieces of news dropped in June 2026. Read them together.

First: Anthropic, OpenAI, and GitHub are shifting from flat-rate subscriptions to consumption-based pricing. Database vendors are lining up to pitch themselves as the solution (The Register).

Second: Gartner warned that AI coding agents will soon cost more than the developers using them. Seat-based licenses are out. Metered billing is in. Engineering teams now face variable, hard-to-predict cost structures on tools they've already baked into their workflow.

Neither story is surprising on its own. Together, they mark a structural shift in how AI tooling gets priced — and it's not moving in your favor.

The flat-rate era was training wheels. You could experiment freely. That window is closing.

The lesson: If you can't meter your AI usage today, you can't manage your AI costs tomorrow.

What to do now:
- Audit every AI tool on consumption-based billing. Know your current baseline.
- Instrument your agents. Treat API calls like infrastructure cost, not software license spend.
- Model the ceiling. What does 10x usage look like on your current plan? Know before your CFO asks.
- Pressure-test alternatives. In June 2026, as vendors raised prices, the local and open-source ecosystem got meaningfully stronger. That's not a coincidence.

The teams that will navigate this best aren't the ones who use the least AI. They're the ones who know exactly what they're running and why.
